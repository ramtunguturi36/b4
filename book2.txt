Chapter 30: The Silent Pull
The days after the internal assessments were supposed to be a time of relief and celebration, but for Srivastav, they felt heavier than the exam week itself. The sweetness that had defined his relationship with Saketh was still there — warm hugs, tender kisses, quiet moments on the terrace — but it now carried an undercurrent of guilt and fear that Srivastav couldn’t shake.
Saketh, unaware of the full depth of Srivastav’s inner turmoil, tried even harder to bring back the effortless joy they had shared before. On Sunday morning, he showed up at Srivastav’s room with breakfast and a bright smile.
“Today is for us,” Saketh announced as he stepped inside and pulled Srivastav into a long, enveloping hug. His strong arms wrapped around him tightly, one hand cradling the back of his head. “No notes, no stress. Just you and me. I missed this version of us.”
They ate breakfast on the bed, legs tangled, feeding each other bites like always. Saketh kept one arm around Srivastav’s shoulders, pressing occasional soft kisses to his temple and cheek. Every touch was gentle and full of love.
“You’ve been a little quiet since the exams,” Saketh said softly, searching Srivastav’s eyes. “I know family pressure is tough, but I’m here. We can talk about it if you want.”
Srivastav forced a smile and leaned into the hug. “It’s nothing. Just adjusting after the exams.”
Saketh accepted the answer for now and tried to lighten the mood. He took Srivastav to the garden in the afternoon, where they sat on their favorite bench. Saketh pulled Srivastav into his lap, arms wrapped securely around his waist, and kissed him slowly — deep, affectionate kisses meant to remind him of their bond.
“I love you,” Saketh whispered between kisses, his hands stroking Srivastav’s back tenderly. “More every day. Let’s not let anything come between us.”
Srivastav kissed him back, trying to lose himself in the moment, but the words from his mother kept echoing: “Be careful about seniors… bad influence… don’t get too close.” The guilt of hiding who he was, combined with the fear of disappointing his conservative family, made every kiss and touch feel bittersweet.
In the evening, they went to the terrace as usual. Saketh had brought the blanket and fairy lights. They lay down together, Srivastav’s head on Saketh’s chest, legs intertwined. Saketh’s arms held him close, one hand gently stroking his hair while the other rested on his lower back.
“Tell me what’s on your mind,” Saketh said quietly, sensing the growing distance. “You’ve been pulling back a little. Is it me? Did I do something wrong?”
Srivastav shook his head quickly. “No… you’ve been perfect. It’s just family. They keep calling and reminding me to focus only on studies. They don’t understand… us.”
Saketh sighed and tightened his hold. “I get it. Parents can be strict. But I’m not asking you to choose between them and me. I just want to be part of your life — supporting you, loving you. We can be careful. We can take things slow if that helps.”
Srivastav stayed silent for a long moment, then whispered, “I love you. I really do. But sometimes I wonder if this is fair to you… or to me.”
Saketh lifted Srivastav’s chin gently and kissed him — a slow, deep kiss full of reassurance and love. “It is fair. Because what we have is real. Don’t let fear take it away from us.”
They stayed on the terrace late into the night, sharing soft kisses and quiet conversations. Saketh kept trying to bridge the growing gap with affection — holding Srivastav closer, whispering “I love you” repeatedly, stroking his hair and back with tender care. Srivastav responded, but his touches were slightly more hesitant, his smiles a little dimmer.
When they finally walked back to the hostel, Saketh gave Srivastav one of their usual long goodnight hugs outside his room.
“I love you,” Saketh whispered, kissing him softly on the lips. “Whatever is bothering you, we’ll face it together. Tomorrow will be better.”
Srivastav hugged him back. “I love you too.”
Inside his room, Srivastav sat on his bed with a heavy heart. He loved Saketh deeply — the way he cared, the way he held him, the way he made him feel seen and safe. But the repeated family calls, the warnings about seniors, and the guilt of hiding his true feelings were creating a growing distance inside him. He felt torn between the love he felt for Saketh and the fear of disappointing his conservative family.
He didn’t sleep well that night.
Saketh, in his own room, felt the shift more clearly now. Srivastav was pulling away emotionally, even if he still said “I love you.” Saketh told himself it was temporary — just family pressure and post-exam fatigue. He would keep showing his love, keep being patient, keep holding him close. He refused to let anything break what they had built.
The sweetness was still there, but it was beginning to feel fragile.
The misunderstanding was slowly deepening, and the emotional distance between them was growing, one quiet moment at a time.
